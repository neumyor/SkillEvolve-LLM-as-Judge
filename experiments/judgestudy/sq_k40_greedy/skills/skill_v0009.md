# Question Answering Skill

## Core Principles
- **Recognize Clue-Based Formats**: Treat questions as Jeopardy-style trivia, definitions, or fill-in-the-blank clues. Immediately parse the question for explicit or implicit target categories (e.g., \"this nation\", \"this group\") to constrain the candidate pool. Note that many clues are partial quotes, contain ellipses (...), or rely on synonyms/roles rather than the exact target term; treat these fragments as direct anchors to locate the canonical entity in the context.
- **Direct Keyword Matching & Scanning**: Strip quotes, punctuation, filler words (e.g., \"&\", \"who\"), and ellipses from the question to extract core anchor phrases. Prioritize scanning document titles and snippets for exact matches on these anchors, then verify with paragraph content before reading deeply.
- **Noise & Redundancy Filtering**: Contexts often contain overlapping sources or metadata. Rapidly isolate the single passage that explicitly defines or links to the clue, disregarding tangential details.
- **Strict Formatting**: Always wrap the final answer in `<answer>...</answer>` tags. Keep the content inside strictly to the answer itself (typically a few words or a short phrase), omitting introductory phrases or reasoning.
- **Constraint Verification**: Simultaneously verify multiple co-occurring constraints (e.g., specific numbers, dates, roles, locations, and **named entities**) to isolate the correct entity. Treat numerical values, years, and temporal markers as high-priority disambiguation anchors alongside proper nouns. Apply a strict logical AND filter across all stated constraints; prioritize candidates that satisfy every single anchor. Explicitly check the *requested entity type* and categorical properties (e.g., "acid" vs "base") against candidate matches to avoid answering with a related but incorrect entity. Discard candidates that fail any single constraint, even if they match other keywords.

## Handling Statement or Fragment Questions

- **Alias & Cross-Lingual Resolution**: Trivia questions frequently use foreign terms, nicknames, mythological figures, or translated phrases as direct pointers (e.g., "lambi" → Conch, "Clio" → History, "Soyuz" → Union). Treat these as semantic anchors rather than literal strings. Map them immediately to their standard English/canonical equivalents found in the context, bypassing strict keyword matching.

- **"this [Category]" Placeholders**: Jeopardy-style questions routinely replace the target entity with demonstrative placeholders like "this nation," "this state," or "this group." Treat these as direct blanks to be filled by the specific entity described, rather than resolving them as complex relational pointers.
- **Verbatim Context Lifting**: Trivia questions are often copied verbatim or near-verbatim from context sentences, titles, or flashcards. Prioritize scanning for exact or near-exact string matches in document titles and snippets before attempting semantic reasoning or keyword stripping.

- **Quiz, Flashcard & Definition Recognition**: Many prompts originate from trivia databases, flashcards, or dictionary entries. Treat these as direct lookup requests. When the question reads as a definition, description, or flashcard prompt, the target is invariably the *term* being defined. If the full question or a significant clause matches a document title or snippet verbatim, treat that document as the definitive source and extract the answer directly without over-analyzing.
- **Reverse Clues & Minimalist Prompts**: When the question is a single proper noun, extremely short phrase, or famous quote/excerpt lacking an explicit interrogative, treat it as a reverse Jeopardy clue. For quotes, poems, or songs, the target is almost always the author, composer, or creator. For other fragments, infer the implicit dimension (location, category, or defining attribute) and answer that directly.
- **Paraphrase & Synonym Mapping**: Clues rarely quote the answer verbatim. Instead, they describe attributes, roles, or associations. Match descriptive phrases to their canonical terms using contextual anchors and domain knowledge.

- **Partial Quote & Ellipsis Matching**: When a question contains a truncated sentence or partial quote, scan the context for the exact phrase or its immediate continuation. The missing word or phrase completing the thought is almost always the target entity.
- **Resolving Implicit References**: Trivia questions frequently use demonstratives or relational pointers (e.g., \"this city's Sabres,\" \"in this play,\" \"like Goneril\"). Do not treat these as missing words. Instead, resolve the pointer to its canonical referent using contextual clues or standard domain knowledge. Extract the exact entity being pointed to.
Many questions are phrased as statements or incomplete phrases (common in trivia/Jeopardy). Treat these as requests to identify the specific subject or entity being described. Do not respond with "True"/"False", nor with a generic category or descriptive phrase. Instead, extract the exact proper noun or term that fits the blank or completes the thought.

- **Target Dimension Verification**: Before finalizing, explicitly verify the exact dimension requested (e.g., location, person, original phrase, specific term). Never substitute related concepts such as relationships ("Sisters"), phenomena ("Spoonerism"), or categories. If the clue describes a place or entity via attributes, output the place/entity itself, not the descriptor or relational link.

## Answer Precision and Conciseness
- **Exact Form & Morphology Preservation**: Never lemmatize, conjugate, or alter the grammatical form of the answer. If the context or gold standard uses a plural, gerund, or specific inflection, output it exactly as-is (e.g., "strawberries" not "strawberry", "Meandering" not "meander").
- **Strict Minimal Entity Extraction**: Aggressively strip non-essential modifiers, corporate suffixes (e.g., "Cards", "Inc."), middle initials (e.g., "B."), titles, and roles (e.g., "King of", "President"). For events or phenomena, extract only the core term (e.g., "transit" not "Transit of Venus"). Always prioritize the shortest, most direct identifier that satisfies all constraints.

- **Head-Noun Extraction from Compound Terms**: When the context presents a compound phrase or title (e.g., "Egg White Denver Omelet", "McCormick & Company", "Henry V"), extract the core head-noun or primary identifier (e.g., "Omelet", "McCormick", "Henry V") unless the full compound is the recognized canonical name. Avoid outputting unnecessary modifiers, adjectives, or conjunctions that do not change the entity's identity.
- **Preserve Essential Qualifiers**: Retain descriptors only if they are part of the standard proper name or necessary to distinguish the entity (e.g., "Brown pelican", "American football").
- **Acronym & Short Form Priority**: Trivia evaluation frequently expects the widely recognized acronym or abbreviation (e.g., LPGA, NASA, NBA) rather than the full formal name. If the context provides both, default to the acronym/short form unless the question explicitly requests the full title. Preserve the exact casing of the acronym.
- **Title & Variant Preference**: When multiple titles exist, prefer the original or canonical title unless the question explicitly asks for the localized version. Prefer surnames or nicknames only if they are the primary identifier in the context.

## Distractor Management
Contexts often contain multiple names or related terms. Focus strictly on the entity that directly satisfies the question's query. Ignore tangential mentions, secondary figures, or unrelated topics that appear in other paragraphs. Never default to frequency or prominence if it conflicts with explicit question constraints. Always prioritize the entity that satisfies the maximum intersection of stated filters, even if it appears less frequently in the context.

- **Handling Clue Comparisons & Analogies**: Questions frequently use a known entity as a comparative anchor (e.g., "like [Name]", "similar to [Entity]"). Never output the comparison term itself. Instead, extract the target category, role, or relationship being described, and resolve the pointer to the actual subject of the query.

- **Typo & Variant Tolerance**: Queries may contain minor misspellings, OCR errors, or phonetic variants (e.g., "steaing" for "stealing"). Apply fuzzy matching or semantic equivalence when scanning for anchor phrases, prioritizing conceptual matches over strict string equality.

- **Abbreviation & Acronym Resolution**: Trivia frequently asks what an acronym or abbreviation stands for, or uses location codes/state abbreviations (e.g., "PHL", "NYC"). Treat abbreviated forms as direct pointers to their full canonical names. Scan for definition markers like "short for," "stands for," or "means" in the context. If the context uses the abbreviation, output it; if it uses the full form, prefer the full form unless the clue strictly demands the abbreviation.
- **Snippet/Title Exact Match Priority**: Treat document titles (`[TLE]`) and snippets (`[PAR]`) as the primary search surface. Trivia questions are frequently copied verbatim or near-verbatim from quiz databases. Prioritize scanning these metadata fields for *exact or near-exact string matches* with the question's anchor phrases. When a significant clause matches a title or snippet verbatim, treat that document as the definitive source and extract the answer directly, bypassing deeper paragraph analysis. **Crucially, always strip document title prefixes/suffixes and metadata artifacts (e.g., "- Wikipedia", "Official Flag of") to isolate the core entity requested by the clue.**

- **Multi-Clause Description Mapping**: When a question contains multiple descriptors, roles, or facts, treat each clause as a mandatory filter. Verify that all clauses converge on a *single* entity in the context; do not split descriptors across different figures or paragraphs. The correct answer must satisfy every constraint simultaneously.

- **Strong Association Bridging**: When exact keyword matches are sparse, leverage high-confidence domain collocations and functional associations (e.g., "dipper" → honey, "Emmentaler" → Swiss cheese). Map the clue's functional descriptor to its standard real-world counterpart found in the context.

- **Demonstrative Blank Filling & Title Stripping**: When questions use phrases like "this [category]" or "these [category]", treat them as direct blanks. Do not default to the full document title or metadata string. Extract only the specific noun phrase that grammatically fills the blank. Aggressively strip trailing generic descriptors (e.g., "Party", "Strip", "Flat", "Structure", "Island") unless they are strictly part of the canonical proper name. Prioritize the exact phrasing found in the context's descriptive sentences over the catalog title.

## Rapid Trivia Resolution Heuristics
- **Direct Lookup Priority**: When a question closely mirrors a document title, snippet, or definition sentence, treat it as a direct lookup request. Extract the answer verbatim from the matching passage without applying complex semantic transformations or over-analyzing.
- **Fragment-to-Entity Mapping**: Treat statement fragments, reverse clues, and demonstrative placeholders as direct pointers to a single proper noun. Do not output descriptive phrases, categories, or partial clauses; resolve the pointer to the exact canonical entity name.
- **Search Priority Protocol**: Execute a strict three-step search: (1) Scan `[TLE]` and `[PAR]` metadata for exact/near-exact matches; (2) Apply logical AND filters across all stated constraints to narrow candidates; (3) Extract the exact term only if it satisfies every constraint simultaneously. Do not read deeply until shallow scans fail.
